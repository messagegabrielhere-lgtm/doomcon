Stress index

0–100 per country, rescored every time a source lands. Three parts, each 0–100:

News: stories from the last 24 hours naming the country. Reports of one event from several outlets are grouped into one story, which counts once plus a quarter per extra outlet (at most double). Weighted by category: military and security 3, nuclear 2.5, unrest and disasters 2, health and humanitarian 1.5, economy, energy, cyber and shipping 1, diplomacy and climate 0.5, other world news 0.4. An outlet writing about its own country (the BBC on Britain, France 24 on France) counts 30%. Then min(100, 16·log₂(1+w)).

Hazard: M4.5+ earthquakes in the last 7 days inside or near the border (energy-weighted), GDACS orange and red alerts, open NASA EONET events.

Market: only where a US-listed country ETF exists. 5-day drawdown plus 20-day volatility against its own year.

Composite = 0.55·News + 0.30·Hazard + 0.15·Market, re-weighted when Market is missing. The arrow is the change over 24 hours, from the hourly history the snapshot keeps. It counts English-language coverage and hazards, not danger: a country nobody writes about scores low.

        World Monitor

        A live map of what the world's newsrooms and hazard feeds are reporting. News from 27 outlets and GDELT is grouped into stories (one event, many outlets). Each country gets a stress index, 0–100, from the stories naming it, natural hazards near it and its stock market. It counts coverage. It is not a forecast.

        Getting around

        Jump (⌘K or /) finds any country, region, layer, preset or action. The region chips on the map fly there and narrow the stories and the index to that region. Click a country for its panel: score, 7-day trend, hazards and stories. ★ a country to watch it: it stays at the top of the index, and with Alerts on you hear when it moves or a big story breaks.

        The slider under the map replays the last 72 hours. Drag it, or press ▶. Stories, hotspots, hazards and the index all rewind with it.
