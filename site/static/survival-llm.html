Survival LLM: a free AI that works with no internet

An AI assistant and a 1.5-million-article survival library that run entirely on your own computer. Ask how to keep food safe in a blackout, treat a burn or purify water, and it answers from its offline library and shows the exact passage each answer came from. Free and open source.

Set it up while you still have internet. Setup takes 20 minutes to about 2½ hours depending on your computer and connection; after that it needs no network at all.

What it does

  • Offline and private. The model and the library live on your computer. Questions never leave it.
  • Shows its sources. Every answer lists the articles it read, with the cited passage highlighted.
  • Reads your files. Add your generator manual, medicines list or family plan (PDF, Word, EPUB, web pages, text).
  • Updates itself when you are online, keeping your models, library and files.

What's in the library

About 930,000 of Wikipedia's most-read articles, WikiMed, MedlinePlus, WikEM, a UK medicines guide, Ready.gov, CDC, U.S. military and FEMA survival and first-aid manuals, the USDA canning guide, wikiHow, iFixit, Appropedia and more.

Everything: 8.8 GB download (19 GB installed). Essentials: 1.3 GB (3 GB installed), without Wikipedia and iFixit.

Install on a Mac

  1. Needs Apple Silicon and macOS 14 Sonoma or newer.
  2. Download Survival-LLM-mac.zip and double-click to unzip.
  3. Double-click Survival LLM. The first time, macOS says it can't check the app for malware, because the app isn't sold through Apple. Click Done; that only closes the warning.
  4. Open the Apple menu › System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security, click Open Anyway next to "Survival LLM was blocked", and confirm with your password or Touch ID. The button shows for about an hour after step 3; if it's gone, repeat step 3.
  5. The app walks you through picking a model and a library.

Install on Windows

  1. Needs 64-bit Windows 10 or 11.
  2. Download Survival-LLM-windows.zip, right-click it and choose Extract All.
  3. Open the extracted folder and double-click Survival LLM.bat. If Windows warns you, click More info › Run anyway.
  4. One click downloads the AI engine (Ollama, 1.6 GB), then a model and a library.

Questions

Who made it?

Ronny Fernandez, released under the MIT license. SIREN did not build it and is not paid by its maker; the only paid links on this page are the Amazon gear links. It is listed here because a cited, offline reference belongs in any bunker kit.

My Mac won't open it. What do I do?

macOS blocks apps that aren't verified by Apple until you approve them once: after the warning, go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. The right-click › Open shortcut no longer works on recent macOS. If the button never appears, open Terminal and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Survival LLM.app" (change the path if the app is still in Downloads), then open the app. Only do this for software you trust; the full source is on GitHub.

Is it medical advice?

No. It is a reference tool that shows its sources so you can check them. In an emergency, call your local emergency number first when you can.
