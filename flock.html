Number-plate cameras, mapped

Flock Safety automated licence-plate-reader cameras, as OpenStreetMap has them

An ALPR is a camera pointed at a road that reads every number plate that passes it. Flock Safety builds them and sells them to police departments, sheriffs' offices, homeowners' associations and retailers. This page is a register of the ones somebody has written down in OpenStreetMap — a public, openly-licensed map that anyone can read and anyone can edit — and it is a register of nothing else.

115,608 cameras, as mapped in OpenStreetMap on 2026-09-26. That is the number of map objects, not the number of cameras. A county with zero mapped cameras is a county nobody has mapped. It is NOT a county with no cameras. This dataset cannot tell those two apart, and neither can anyone reading it: 948 of 3,235 counties are in that state.

Read this before you read the map

A county with zero mapped cameras is a county nobody has mapped. It is NOT a county with no cameras. This dataset cannot tell those two apart, and neither can anyone reading it: 948 of 3,235 counties are in that state.

Coverage follows volunteers, not deployments. A city where a DeFlock contributor has been active looks saturated; an identical city with no contributor looks empty. The difference between them is the contributor.

Flock Safety publishes its own deployment figures and they differ from this count. This site does not reconcile the two, because reconciling them would mean imputing cameras nobody has mapped, and this site never imputes.

So the only claim any figure on this page makes is the one in its own caption: this many cameras are written down in OpenStreetMap on 2026-09-26. Comparing two regions on this page is partly comparing two regions' mapping effort, and nothing here separates the two.

The map 115,306

Every camera is binned into a cell 0.25° on a side — about 28 km of latitude — and each occupied cell is drawn as its own projected quadrilateral, shaded by how many are in it. Land with no mapped camera is hatched, not left blank, because a blank would be an assertion this data cannot make. The picture needs no JavaScript and every figure in it is a number in the tables below. With it, the map pans and zooms, reads out any cell you point at, and — once you are in close — offers the individual cameras, each with the way its lens points.

How the bearing was written

OpenStreetMap accepts 4 forms in this field and they are not equally solid. One of them is derived rather than tagged, and a page that folded them all together would be publishing a computation as an observation.

  • 105,107 bearing a plain bearing in degrees, e.g. "142".
  • 4,141 multi an OSM multi-value, e.g. "0;0". The first value is used; the others are not averaged in.
  • 3,908 sector an OSM field-of-view arc, e.g. "338-23" — 45 degrees wide, centred on 0. The centre is DERIVED, not tagged.
  • 58 compass a 16-point compass letter, e.g. "NNE".

4,053 cameras state a field of view as well as a bearing — most commonly 45° wide, on 3,650 of them. Only the sector form states a field of view. Where it does, the width is real; where it does not, no width is inferred. Widths are not carried per camera in flock-points.json — they are aggregate only.

What this is not

Published by the collector, printed here unedited.

  • Every figure here counts cameras as mapped in OpenStreetMap on 2026-09-26. It is not a count of cameras that exist.
  • 948 of 3,235 counties have zero mapped cameras. That means nobody has mapped them, not that they have none.
  • OpenStreetMap coverage is volunteer-driven and uneven; the map is partly a map of where mappers have been.
  • Direction is tagged on 113,241 of 115,608 cameras. The untagged ones are shown as untagged, never as facing north.
  • 5,741 cameras carry a bare direction=0. That is both "due north" and what a mapper leaves behind when the bearing is unknown, so the north bin is inflated by an amount nobody can measure. It is not reweighted.
  • An operating agency is recorded for 14,953 of 115,608. For the rest OSM does not say who runs the camera, and neither does this page.
  • County assignment uses TIGER polygons simplified to about 550 m, so a camera within a few hundred metres of a county line can land in the neighbouring county.
  • 258 mapped cameras fall outside any US county. They are counted and kept, not dropped.
  • Flock Safety publishes different deployment figures. The two are not reconciled here, because reconciling them would mean imputing.

This is a snapshot and not a time series. Nothing here says when a camera was installed or whether it is switched on — only that an OpenStreetMap object exists. A camera taken down keeps its tag until somebody visits and removes it.

Where this came from

OpenStreetMap ALPR nodes tagged Flock Safety, via Overpass. Every camera here is an OpenStreetMap object tagged man_made=surveillance and surveillance:type=ALPR, carrying at least one of manufacturer=Flock Safety, brand=Flock Safety, operator=Flock Safety. The three are unioned rather than taken one at a time, because they overlap: 1,652 objects carry more than one of them and would otherwise be counted twice. OpenStreetMap's own snapshot clock for this extract reads 2026-09-26T03:52:04Z; this page was generated 2026-09-26 03:58:53 UTC.

Substitute a bounding box into query_template and POST it to the endpoint as a form-encoded `data=` field (a raw POST body answers HTTP 406). Every figure on this page is the arithmetic in collector/flock.mjs over that response and nothing else.

The harvest

A single United States query is about 42 MB and times out, so the planet was tiled: 5 seed boxes covering it exactly once with no gap and no overlap, the contiguous states pre-cut into a grid. 36 data queries and 5 count queries were issued to one endpoint; 0 tiles needed splitting, 0 levels of recursion were used and 0 duplicates were absorbed at tile edges.

Tiles are fetched with no `out` limit, so a tile either arrives whole or fails loudly — there is no silent truncation to detect. One count query per seed box cross-checks the result: seed_cross_checks compares the box total against the distinct cameras its tiles yielded, and a shortfall over 2% aborts the run rather than publishing a partial map.

Geography

Counties and states come from Census TIGERweb county boundaries, 3,235 county polygons simplified to about 0.005° — roughly 550 m. 3,314 cameras sit close enough to a county line that the assignment could belong to the neighbour, and 258 fall outside any US county and are kept rather than dropped.

Take the data

Both files are published beside this page, byte-identical to what the collector wrote — the build copies them verbatim rather than re-serialising, so the checksum of the file in the repository is the checksum of the file served here.

  • /api/flock.json Every number on this page: the totals, the coverage block, all 3,235 counties including the 948 with none mapped, and the 5,029-cell grid this map is drawn from.
  • /api/flock-points.json 115,608 individual cameras as four packed integer arrays — latitude, longitude, bearing and state. 2,672 KiB. A bearing of −1 means no usable direction tag, not north. The page never inlines it. The map offers to fetch it once you are zoomed past 12× — a button, not an ambush, because 0.9 MB over the wire is the reader's decision — and past 40× draws each camera with its bearing.

Recompute it

Substitute a bounding box for the four zeroes and POST it to https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter as a form-encoded data= field. A raw POST body answers HTTP 406.

[out:json][timeout:600];
(
  nwr["man_made"="surveillance"]["surveillance:type"="ALPR"]["manufacturer"="Flock Safety"](0.00000,0.00000,0.00000,0.00000);
  nwr["man_made"="surveillance"]["surveillance:type"="ALPR"]["brand"="Flock Safety"](0.00000,0.00000,0.00000,0.00000);
  nwr["man_made"="surveillance"]["surveillance:type"="ALPR"]["operator"="Flock Safety"](0.00000,0.00000,0.00000,0.00000);
);
