What the people running AI said on the record in the last 7 days, in the words their publications printed. 2 of 15 are on the record this week across 3 lines; the other 13 are listed too, because a name missing from a list and a name with nothing beside it are different facts. Compiled 2026-09-26 16:21:54 UTC from the same 200-item corpus as the signal feed.

What this page never does

It never writes a quotation. Not a paraphrase, not a summary, not a tightened version of what somebody said. The only words attributed to a named person anywhere on this page are the headline, reproduced exactly as the publication printed it and linked to that publication. There is no transcript here, so there is no quotation here.

Attributing invented words to a living person is the one error this site could not recover from. A wrong number is checkable against a published formula; an invented sentence is checkable against nothing, and the person has to deny it. Headline-only means every line below can be verified in one click, by anyone, including by the person named in it.

A line is a headline that names one of these fifteen people and carries a speech cue — said, told, interview, keynote, letter, or words in quotation marks. The cue is printed on every line, and its class matters: a document cue means a letter or a memo named the person, which may be by them or about them. NO LINE means no headline in this 200-item corpus named that person beside a cue. It is not a claim that the person said nothing, and the matching vocabulary — including the 11 near-synonyms added beyond the published list — is in the data file.

The roster

The mark beside each name is an abstract monogram — a geometric emblem keyed to the person's initials and their organisation. It is not a portrait, not a likeness and not a caricature, and no photograph of any person appears anywhere on this site. The shape tells you which organisation; the name printed beside it is the identification, and you are never asked to recognise a face.

How a line gets here

The test
A headline in data/news.json is a line when it names a roster member — whole word, against the published alias table below — and carries a speech cue. Both conditions, every time. 1 headline in this window named one of these people and carried no cue, and it was excluded rather than printed as speech.
The cues
34 core: say · says · said · saying · tell · tells · told · telling · warn · warns · warned · warning · warnings · argue · argues · argued · arguing · interview · interviews · interviewed · Q and A · Q&A · testify · testifies · testified · testimony · podcast · podcasts · keynote · keynotes · letter · letters · memo · memos, plus any words the publication put in double quotation marks in its own headline. 11 extended, and this is an editorial judgement rather than a measurement: talk · talks · talked · talking · speak · speaks · spoke · speaking · speech · quoted · remarks. Every line records which list caught it.
Cue classes
verb — the headline's own verb is an act of speech
appearance — the headline names a venue or a format — interview, podcast, keynote, testimony
document — a document names the person. It may be by them or about them; the headline is the only thing that can tell you, and it is printed in full
quote — the publication put words in quotation marks in its own headline
The roster
Fifteen people, fixed order, never pruned. The matching vocabulary is data, not a heuristic: no fuzzy matching, no edit distance, no initial inference. If a spelling is not in the table it does not match.

Two names we deliberately do not match
Bare Huang and bare Liang are absent from the table. Both are among the commonest surnames on earth and this corpus carries chip-supply and research headlines by the hundred; a bare match would put a stranger's words under a named living person's row. Jensen and Wenfeng are distinctive enough to keep. For the same reason a bare surname preceded by a different given name is ruled out — Daniela Amodei is not Dario Amodei.
The window
7 days, 200 of 200 items scanned, oldest 2026-09-24. The corpus is capped at 200 items and on a busy week the cap binds long before the window does, so this is the last 200 items rather than a full seven days of everything published.
What it cannot see
Everything these fifteen people said that no outlet in our source list put in a headline. A podcast nobody wrote up, a post on X, a remark in a filing — all invisible here. 13 of 15 rows read no line this week, and that is a statement about this corpus, not about those people.

AI Early Warning System

DOOMCON measures how much is happening, not how bad it is. Levels describe observable activity tempo against this index’s own record. They are not a probability of harm.

  • WattsThe substrate index: grid load, drought and buildout under the models.
  • MapWhere the compute physically sits, against the water it needs.

  • BlissThe same machinery, pointed the other way.
