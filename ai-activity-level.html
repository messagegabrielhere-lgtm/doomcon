How do you check a SIREN reading yourself?

Every reading is published with a receipt: its inputs, its score, and the hash of the receipt before it. Press the button and your own browser fetches the last 12 receipts, re-hashes each one and walks the chain. Nothing is sent anywhere, and no other doom number lets you do this.

What this proves: the published record has not been edited after the fact. What it does not prove: that the inputs were right. For that, read How the SIREN AI activity index is computed · methodology and the source. The files: this reading’s receipt · every reading.

What is SIREN, and what does the name stand for?

SIREN is an hourly index of how much is happening in AI. SI is superintelligence and REN is Real-time Early Notice: a siren for the road to superintelligence. It counts public activity across five pillars (capability, compute & capital, attention, governance, markets), turns it into one score from 0 to 100, and reports a level from 5 (quietest) to 1 (loudest). It was called DOOMCON until October 2026; the method and the record are unchanged.

Does SIREN say superintelligence is here, or coming?

No. It does not detect superintelligence and it does not forecast it. It measures how loud AI activity is right now: releases, compute, headlines, rule-making and market prices. A loud reading means a lot is happening, and nothing more.

Is there an AI doomsday clock I can verify?

Most AI doomsday clocks are expert judgement. SIREN is different: an hourly activity count from public data, with a receipt for every reading. The Bulletin's Doomsday Clock is not AI-specific. See Is there an AI doomsday clock? and What is p(doom)? for the comparison.

Is SIREN like DEFCON or the Doomsday Clock?

It borrows DEFCON's grammar, a scale that counts down from 5 to 1. The difference is what sits behind the number. DEFCON is set by military command and the Doomsday Clock by a board's judgement; SIREN is arithmetic over public data, and every reading is published with the inputs needed to recompute it. Sites that put DEFCON or Doomsday Clock in an AI title for traffic are brand hijack — see SIREN vs PizzINT: which should you use? and How does SIREN compare to DEFCON, the Doomsday Clock and p(doom)?.

Is SIREN an alternative to DoomBench, Skynet Countdown or PizzINT?

Only if you want a recomputable activity count instead of a judgement-scored doom, timeline number, or DEFCON-shaped novelty page. DoomBench scores takeover evidence; Skynet Countdown estimates AGI dates and control-loss odds; PizzINT borrows DEFCON/Doomsday framing; SIREN counts how loud AI activity is this hour. Does a louder level mean AI is more dangerous?

No. The level measures tempo: releases, compute, headlines, rule-making and market prices. A loud week means a lot happened. It says nothing about whether what happened was good or bad, and the site never sums harm and benefit into one figure.

How is the level calculated, and how often?

A collector runs every hour, scores each pillar against the index's own frozen record, and combines them into the composite. The level is the band the score falls in: 5 DORMANT (0–34), 4 ROUTINE (35–54), 3 ELEVATED (55–69), 2 ACCELERATED (70–84), 1 UNPRECEDENTED (85–100). The formulas and constants are on the methodology page.

Can I check the number myself?

Yes. Each reading ships with a receipt holding its inputs, its score and the hash of the receipt before it, and the state, history and receipts are open JSON. If a source did not report, the page says so and no value is filled in for it.

Who runs SIREN, and how is it paid for?

One person runs it. It is free. Visits are counted with Google Analytics and cookieless GoatCounter, both disclosed on the privacy page. The paid links are the Amazon links on the reading list and in the Bunker Kit's supplies crate, which are labelled; there is room for one named sponsor with no say over the number, and a donate link to the project's X account.
